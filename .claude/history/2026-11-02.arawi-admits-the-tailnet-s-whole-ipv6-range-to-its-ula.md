# arawi: the tailnet's whole IPv6 range reaches its ULA (2026-11-02)

- arawi's `tailnet_subnet_ipv6` alias held `fd7a:115c:a1e0:ab12::/64`, the shape of older Tailscale IPv6 addresses. Nodes now have addresses such as `fd7a:115c:a1e0::ae01:c388`, outside it, so pfSense's default deny (rule `1000000103`) dropped their IPv6 to arawi's ULA. The alias is now `fd7a:115c:a1e0::/48`, as arawi's Tailnet IPv6 route has it.
- The subnet routers forward with SNAT off, so a packet's source tells which alias admits it. tailscalerouter reaches `fd83:b84c:aa57:1::/64` over `tailscale0`, from its tailnet address. The VCN's other instances route there through tailscalerouter, from their own GUAs, which `vcn_subnet_ipv6` already admitted; arawi answered them throughout.
- arawi's dropped packets show in `/var/log/filter.log`, readable only by root: `sudo grep <source> /var/log/filter.log` on arawi, whose login shell is csh, so pipes and redirects go through `sh -c`.
- pfsensible's `pfsense_rule` can't take an IPv6 literal as a source ("Cannot parse address"); rules use aliases, as arawi's TODO.md records.
- With the alias widened, the VCN's resolver forwards the homelab's zones to arawi's ULA ahead of its IPv4 address. `unbound-control dump_infra` shows both with measured round trips and no timeouts.
