# prodingus's scheduled reboot hung and panicked; containers now stop before their storage and start after it (2026-11-16)

gonkputer's guests were down from 02:02 to 02:57. unattended-upgrades had installed a Proxmox kernel and scheduled the reboot for 02:00 (`Automatic-Reboot-Time`). The shutdown hung, the hung-task check panicked the node at about 02:08, and kdump ran until 02:47.

- **Why the shutdown hung.** On a node shutdown, pve-ha-lrm stops each HA container through `PVE::HA::Resources::PVECT::shutdown`: a `vm_shutdown` with a 60-second timeout and no `forceStop`. A container that does not stop is logged as `unable to stop service ct:<id> (still running)` and left running. `pve-container@.service` sets `DefaultDependencies=No`, so systemd never stops it either. Ceph then stopped under timemachine's mounted RBD root (`rbd0`). `systemd-shutdown`'s sync hung on it (`jbd2/rbd0-8`, `kmmpd-rbd0`), and 122 seconds later `kernel.hung_task_panic` panicked the node. The 02:00 reboot on 2026-11-06 had the same unstopped timemachine without a panic.
- **What kdump cost.** On 7.0.14-28, kdump (makedumpfile 1.7.6, `-d 31`, xz, `nr_cpus=1`) was at 35.3% after 8.7 GiB and 38 minutes when the root filesystem filled, which puts a full dump near 25 GiB and two hours. Nov 1's complete dump, on 7.0.14-26, was 8.6 GiB in 37 minutes. makedumpfile warns "The kernel version is not supported." kdump-tools purges old dumps only after a successful save, and counts the `2*` entries in `KDUMP_COREDIR`.
- **Why the disk was full.** The root also holds cachefilesd's cache of the CephFS (`fsc`). cachefilesd compares the filesystem's free space for non-root users against percentages of all its blocks. It filled the root down to its limits (then `bstop 6%`, `bcull 14%`, `brun 20%`), and after boot it culled back up to `brun`. It reads its configuration only as it starts; a restart withdraws the cache and binds it again.
- **Why timemachine was stacked.** Proxmox mounts CephFS at storage activation by writing `/run/systemd/system/mnt-pve-cephfs.mount` and starting it. At boot the first attempt fails (exit 32) while the OSDs come up, and pvestatd's next activation, about 30 seconds later, succeeds. The HA manager starts containers in between, and Proxmox activates no storage for a host-path mount point (`mp0: /mnt/pve/cephfs,…`). Containers that started in that window bound the host's empty directory, with the CephFS stacked over it later (ext4 under ceph at `/mnt/cephfs`). timemachine's Quadlet failed six times at the 2026-11-06 boot (`statfs /mnt/cephfs/application/timemachine: no such file or directory`), so Time Machine was down until 2026-11-16. qb binds a subdirectory, which does not exist before the mount, so its start fails instead.
- **The stacked mount stalls systemd 259's shutdown.** This is systemd 259.9, in Ubuntu 26.04 privileged containers, reproduced on tvheadend.
  - After `mnt-cephfs.mount` unmounted, all 29 shutdown jobs stayed `waiting`. `network-online.target`'s stop was held back by no job, yet never ran.
  - `systemctl stop network-online.target` inside the container re-queued it, and the shutdown finished within a second.
  - Unstacked, the same container stopped in 10 seconds.
  - tvheadend unmounted `mnt-cephfs.mount` only after `sysinit.target` had stopped, beside the local mounts. immich, also stacked, unmounted it a second after the stop signal, and stopped cleanly twice.
- **Watching a container stop from the host.**
  - Every container's persisted journal ends at `Stopping systemd-journal-flush.service`: there journald moves to `/run/log/journal`.
  - The host reads that volatile journal with `journalctl --file="/proc/<init>/root/run/log/journal/*/*.journal"` while the container lives. Its init is in `/sys/fs/cgroup/lxc/<id>/ns/init.scope`.
  - `systemctl list-jobs --after` inside it shows which jobs hold back which.
- **HA's error state.** `pct shutdown` of an HA resource becomes `ha-manager crm-command stop <sid> <timeout>`. A stop past its timeout leaves the resource in `error`. Recovery is `ha-manager set <sid> --state disabled`, then `--state started`.
- **Ordering guests against storage.** `pvesm status` activates each storage (`storage_info` calls `activate_storage` in an `eval`). A CephFS storage reports `active` only while mounted. pve-ha-lrm `Wants` and is ordered `After` `pve-storage.target`, which is ordered after every Ceph target and `remote-fs.target`. pveproxy and pvescheduler also start after it. Proxmox stops containers through their command socket, never `systemctl stop`, so ordering their stop jobs after pve-guests and pve-ha-lrm cannot deadlock.
- **What changed.**
  - A `pve-container@.service.d` drop-in (proxmox_cluster) stops any container left running after pve-guests and pve-ha-lrm, through the unit's `lxc-stop`, before `pve-storage.target`.
  - `pve-storage-active@<storage>.service` holds `pve-storage.target`, for up to 5 minutes, until `pvesm` reports the storage active. It is enabled for `cephfs` in prodingus's `proxmox_cluster_guest_storages`.
  - A cachefilesd role owns cachefilesd's whole configuration. prodingus leaves 2 × 25 GiB free: `bstop 54%`, with `bcull` and `brun` raised by the same 48 points.
  - kdump-mail mails each crash dump that has not yet been mailed, at the next boot. It reads the dump directory from each tool's configuration: Oracle Linux's kdump writes to `/var/oled/crash`, for which AIDE has no rule.
- **Not reached.** pihole2 and riko have no `known_hosts` entry on the WSL controller, so their applies failed as unreachable, though alock recorded them. They lack kdump-mail.
