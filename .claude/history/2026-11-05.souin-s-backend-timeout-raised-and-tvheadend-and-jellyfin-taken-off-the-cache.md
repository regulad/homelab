# Souin's backend timeout raised, and tvheadend and Jellyfin taken off the cache (2026-11-05)

- Since every site on ingress imported `cache` (2026-10-28), Caddy cut any response still running after 10 seconds, logging `context deadline exceeded` and `aborting with incomplete response` against the upstream. Those errors went from at most 15 a day to 1,305 on 10-29. By 11-05 they had hit nextcloud 1,528 times, hass-livingston 70, ai 8, tvheadend-livingston 5 and jf 3.
- In Souin v1.7.9 (cache-handler v0.17.0), `context/timeout.go` gives every request the handler sees a `context.WithTimeout` of `default_cache.timeout.backend`, in `SetBaseContext`, before any bypass: WebSocket upgrades, `text/event-stream`, uncached methods and mutations carry it to the upstream too. A backend of 0, or one `time.ParseDuration` cannot parse, means the 10s default, so it cannot be turned off; the global `cache` block now sets `timeout { backend 8760h }`. Caddy logs `Set backend timeout to …` per site at each load.
- Souin also holds every GET it handles until the upstream finishes: it hands the upstream a `CustomWriter` whose `Write` only appends to a buffer, and `Send` writes the whole body afterwards, as one piece or through `http.ServeContent` for a range request. Only WebSocket upgrades, `text/event-stream` requests and paths matching its `exclude` regex go straight to the upstream. A live stream therefore never reaches the client: with the timeout raised, tvheadend's streams still ended when the client gave up, libmpv after 10 seconds and Chrome after 20, which Caddy logs as `context canceled`. A large download waits whole in Caddy's memory before its first byte leaves, so a throttled client still receives it steadily once it is buffered. tvheadend-livingston and jf no longer import `cache`; a response from a site without it carries no `Cache-Status` header.
- ingress's `caddy.service` rebuilds Caddy with xcaddy at every start, with its plugins unpinned, so a restart can bring in another Souin; a reload does not rebuild.
