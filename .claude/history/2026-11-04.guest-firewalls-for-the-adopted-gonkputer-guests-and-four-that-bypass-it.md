# Guest firewalls for the adopted gonkputer guests, and four that bypass it (2026-11-04)

- pve-firewall 6.0.6 (`ruleset_create_vm_chain`) drops a guest's outbound router advertisements unless `radv` is on, and its `dhcp` option admits only a DHCP client's traffic (in from 67 to 68, out from 68 to 67): a DHCP server needs a rule of its own. The guest chain filters everything entering the guest's NIC, routed traffic included, so a router behind an input policy of `DROP` drops what it forwards.
- The provider's delete of `proxmox_virtual_environment_firewall_options` only clears state: the guest's `/etc/pve/firewall/<vmid>.fw` stays on the node as it was.
- OpenTofu accepts a `dynamic "rule"` block whose `for_each` is a map with one sensitive element, `local.dual_stack_internal_sources`, whose IPv6 list includes the GUA variables; only that rule's source shows as sensitive in the plan.
- tvheadend takes its HDHomeRun (10.23.1.6) through the `tvhdhomerun` frontend: the tuner sends each stream to a UDP port tvheadend binds at random, and answers discovery broadcasts from its own address, neither of which conntrack matches to anything tvheadend sent. Its SAT>IP server is off (`satip_rtsp: 0`), so its SSDP socket is only its own client discovery.
- UniFi OS Server on unifi runs in rootless Podman, so its ports are held by `pasta`; the host's own `discovery` process listens on UDP 10001. Ubiquiti's port reference answers automated fetches with 403.
- UrBackup binds its discovery socket, UDP 35623, on each of its addresses, link-local included; its clients answer a broadcast from their own addresses, which conntrack does not match to the broadcast.
- Rootful Podman's published ports show as `conmon` listeners in `ss`, and only while the container that publishes them runs.
- Ingress's Caddy reaches every backend whose firewall admits only ingress through `dynamic a <host> <port> { versions ipv4 }`, as it reaches aboriginal. Over IPv6 it would connect from its EUI-64 GUA, which no firewall can list. kasm's upstream keeps TLS through `tls` in its HTTP transport, since a `dynamic` upstream takes no `https://` scheme.
- The provider applies a change to a running container's `network_interface`, such as its `firewall` flag, by rebooting the container through `POST .../status/reboot`, and the apply waits for that task. tvheadend's clean shutdown then stalled for over 15 minutes, every remaining systemd job waiting and none running, behind `lxc-stop --timeout 1800`; `pct exec 127 -- systemctl --force --force poweroff` ended it, and the reboot task started the container again. A VM's NIC flag (vkasm's) changed without a reboot.
- Uptime Kuma's monitors and their latest results can be read from its embedded MariaDB, the fastest way to find what a firewall change cut off: `podman exec uptime-kuma mariadb --socket=/app/data/run/mariadb.sock kuma` on uptime, tables `monitor` and `heartbeat`. cordpepperer and peppercord are checked at peppercord's `http://<host>.regulad.internal:8080/health`, and the uptime watcher restarts a container whose check fails, every ten minutes while it does.
