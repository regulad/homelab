# wako resolves and answers mDNS through NetworkManager and resolved, and arawi serves FreeIPA's records (2026-11-04)

- **systemd-resolved is fully supported from RHEL 10.0** (its 10.0 release notes), DNSSEC validation aside, which stays a Technology Preview; in RHEL 8 and 9 the whole service was one. The notes name it an mDNS resolver and responder. AlmaLinux 10 ships it as `systemd-resolved` in BaseOS. Avahi is in BaseOS too, but `nss-mdns` is in no Enterprise Linux repository but EPEL, so Avahi there could answer mDNS but not resolve `.local` names for programs.
- **NetworkManager's plugin for resolved ignores global DNS.** `dns=systemd-resolved` in `[main]` (RHEL 10's networking guide, 19.2) hands resolved each link's servers and domains from its profile, DHCP and router advertisements, the upstream router's address among them; `[global-dns]` and `[global-dns-domain-*]` reach NetworkManager's own `no-stub-resolv.conf` and its DnsManager's `Configuration`, but `update()` in 1.56's `nm-dns-systemd-resolved.c` never reads the `global_config` it is passed. A profile's `connection.mdns` works only with that plugin. wako's NetworkManager is kept out of DNS instead (`dns=none`, `systemd-resolved=false`), and resolved, which no one tells about the links, gives each its global settings: `MulticastDNS=`, and `DNS=` and `Domains=` in its own drop-in.
- **resolved reads a foreign `/etc/resolv.conf`** into its global DNS servers while that is not its own stub: started before the stub link, it kept NetworkManager's three nameservers as global ones until it restarted.
- **Proxmox writes an Enterprise Linux 10 container's NetworkManager profile** at each start, `/etc/NetworkManager/system-connections/<name>.nmconnection`, with a new UUID each time and the node's nameservers by family (`setup_network_with_networkmanager` in `PVE/LXC/Setup/CentOS.pm`; releases before 10 get `ifcfg` files). `/etc/.pve-ignore.<name>.nmconnection` keeps it from the file, so the role leaves the profile to Proxmox and puts its settings in `conf.d`.
- **FreeIPA without its own DNS** writes the records it needs, in zone-file format, to `/tmp/ipa.system.records.<random>.db` at install. `ipa dns-update-system-records --dry-run` lists them afresh, from `IPASystemRecords.get_base_records()` and `get_locations_records()` in `ipaserver.dns_data_management`, which root on the server can call through the server API, as ldap2 binds over ldapi as root. wako, the only server, with the roles IPA master and CA server, needs SRV records for Kerberos, kpasswd and LDAP, the realm's TXT and URI records, and `ipa-ca`. AD records come only with `ipa-adtrust-install`.
- **arawi's Unbound 1.26.1** parses URI records in `local-data` (`unbound-checkconf` on a config piped to `/dev/stdin`, with a record of an unknown type as the negative control). A `local-data` CNAME in a static zone comes back bare, which glibc's resolver does not follow, and in a `redirect` zone with its target resolved (as `mynetworksettings.com`'s): `ipa-ca` has a `redirect` zone of its own, holding a CNAME to wako, whose addresses DHCP and `ndp-aaaa.php` publish. arawi's root shell is tcsh, so a script for it goes through `sh`.
- **zsh does not split an unquoted variable into words:** `dig @arawi $q`, with `q="wako.regulad.internal A"`, asks for one name ending in " A" and gets NXDOMAIN.
