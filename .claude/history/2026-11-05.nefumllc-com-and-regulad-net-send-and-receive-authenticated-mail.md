# nefumllc.com and regulad.net send and receive authenticated mail (2026-11-05)

- postsrsd rewrote the envelope sender of all mail from a domain other than its own, regulad.xyz, submitted mail included: `pw@nefumllc.com` left as `SRS0=…=nefumllc.com=pw@regulad.xyz`. Rspamd's `dkim_signing` signs only when the envelope sender's domain matches the From header's, so nothing from either domain had been DKIM-signed, and its SPF result was for regulad.xyz, unaligned. Gmail refused such a message from regulad.net with `550 5.7.26` under its `p=reject` DMARC policy. `SRS_EXCLUDE_DOMAINS` is now mail's three domains, `regulad.xyz,regulad.net,nefumllc.com`; it named only `mail.regulad.xyz`, an old workaround.
- Rspamd signs and ARC-seals nefumllc.com and regulad.net, selector `mail`, with new 2048-bit keys from OpenSSL's `genpkey` (PKCS#8, as the existing key is), written into mail's SOPS file without being printed. nefumllc.com's earlier key had never signed anything: `dkim_signing.conf` named only regulad.xyz. With `check_pubkey = true`, Rspamd signs only while the key in DNS matches the file.
- After the change, Gmail's `Authentication-Results` for a message from each domain read `dkim=pass`, `spf=pass` (mail's IPv6 address, the envelope sender kept) and `dmarc=pass`. Both DMARC policies are `p=reject`. A message injected with the container's `sendmail` is signed like a submitted one. The Gmail connector cannot read spam, even with `in:spam`.
- OCI Email Delivery has an email domain and DKIM key for each, and approves `pw@nefumllc.com` and `pw@regulad.net`, the only addresses at either domain, for Postfix's fallback relay. Oracle refuses an approved sender until the domain's DKIM key is `ACTIVE`, and moved both from `NEEDS_ATTENTION`/`NEED_DNS` to `ACTIVE` within an hour of their CNAMEs appearing in DNS, so the senders came in a second apply. Email Delivery refuses an unapproved envelope sender with `535 … Envelope From address … not authorized`, which `postfix-reply-filter.regexp` turns into a `451` deferral for all three domains. `tofu/oracle`'s `email_dkim` output is a map by domain.
- regulad.net has its MX, SPF (`v=spf1 mx include:rp.oracleemaildelivery.com -all`, as each domain now has), DKIM key, Email Delivery's DKIM CNAME, and DMARC reporting to Cloudflare's DMARC Management. Provider 5.28.1 has no resource for DMARC Management; `PATCH /zones/{zone_id}/email/auth/dmarc-reports` with `enabled: true` turned it on, through the Cloudflare MCP server. A `GET` of that endpoint creates the zone's configuration, with its report address prefix. Enabling it with the `_dmarc` record already naming that address left the record as it was.
- nefumllc.com and regulad.net are signed with DNSSEC, `cloudflare_zone_dnssec`. Cloudflare Registrar, their registrar, submits the DS records itself, through CDS/CDNSKEY scanning, which Cloudflare says takes one to two days; until then the resources read `pending` against the configuration's `active`, and plans of other changes leave them out with `-exclude`.
- MTA-STS works per recipient domain, so each of the three publishes `_mta-sts` and serves the policy at `mta-sts.<domain>`. A Worker, `mta-sts`, serves it through a custom domain in each zone, whose certificate Cloudflare issues. The policy is in `mode: testing`, with CRLF line ends, and its id, which the TXT records name, is a hash of it, so any change to the policy changes them. regulad.xyz's earlier record was wrapped in curly quotes, `\226\128\156` and `\226\128\157`, and named a policy no host served.
- No MX record exists for `mail.regulad.xyz`; its SPF record covers the HELO name.
