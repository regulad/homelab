# doppelgangerhq.com at 4 OCPUs and 12 GB, with the website and the devices on CPUs of their own (2026-11-14)

- A Redroid device may use two CPUs (`cpus: "2.0"` in the compose file the app writes), and it draws its screen in software (`redroid_gpu_mode=guest`) and encodes its stream on the CPU; on the old two-OCPU shape one booting device took the whole VM from the web app. Android 12 booted to `sys.boot_completed` in 34 s there.
- Oracle resizes a running Flex instance in place: same instance, IPs, VNICs and attachments, with a reboot as part of the update (2 min 20 s). A volume's VPUs change while it is attached (8 s each). The plan shows `oci_core_ipv6.web`'s `vnic_id` as "known after apply", because the instance's change defers the `oci_core_vnic_attachments` lookup to apply time; it resolved to the same VNIC and the IPv6 was not updated.
- Oracle's price list gives a paid tenancy the first 3,000 OCPU hours and 18,000 GB hours of A1 a month, about 4 OCPUs and 24 GB; the Always Free page's 1,500 and 9,000 are for Always Free tenancies.
- On four CPUs the instance's devices change: virtio-scsi has a request queue per CPU, whose interrupts the kernel's managed affinity keeps on their own CPUs (`struct irq_affinity desc = { .pre_vectors = 2 }` in `virtio_scsi.c`: only config, control and event move), and virtio_net comes up with two queue pairs. Whenever its channel count is set, virtio_net spreads the online CPUs over its active pairs and writes each pair's share as its IRQs' affinity, over `irqaffinity=`'s default: CPUs 0-1 and 2-3 for two pairs, all four for one. `CombinedChannels=1` in a drop-in for netplan's `/run/systemd/network/10-netplan-enp0s6.link` (`/etc/systemd/network/10-netplan-enp0s6.link.d/`), which udev applies as the NIC appears, so put every network interrupt on CPUs 2-3 at the next boot; irqbalance with `IRQBALANCE_BANNED_CPULIST` (1.9.3 in noble), from a drop-in for its unit, moves them back and keeps them off the devices' CPUs. Ubuntu's 6.8 kernel has no `/sys/kernel/debug/irq`, so a read cannot tell a managed interrupt from another.
- `irqaffinity=` is independent of the CPU count, so it went in before the resize and took effect at the resize's reboot. The devices' slice is counted from facts, so it went in after.
- With the systemd cgroup driver, dockerd's `cgroup-parent` is a slice name (default `system.slice`), and a container's own `cgroup_parent` takes precedence; containers the app creates from its own compose files land in the daemon's slice. A container without one of its own takes the daemon's current default each time it starts.
- systemd 255 applied `AllowedCPUs=` to the running `system.slice` and `user.slice` at `daemon-reload`, without a restart. The slices were written before dockerd restarted with its new parent, so that no container started in an implicit slice without a CPU set.
- `ansible_processor_nproc` counts the CPUs the gathering process may run on, which `user.slice`'s CPU set narrows; `ansible_processor_vcpus` counts `/proc/cpuinfo`.
- PM2's `instances: "max"` counts every CPU of the host, not the container's CPU set: the app runs four workers on its two CPUs.
- After a boot, `web` can exit once as PM2's IPC channel drops while it fails to reach PM2 Plus; `restart: unless-stopped` brings it back.
